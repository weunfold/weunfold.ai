Initiative · Shared compute

Compute we can build together.

A proposal to bring together the resources to acquire and operate a shared B300 cluster, with transparent costs, shared participation, and a commitment to widening access.

Stage: proposalDetails in development

Most people who could build something useful with AI can't buy the machines it runs on. Shared compute lets researchers, maintainers, small teams and communities run real work on capacity whose costs and rules they can see and help shape.

B300 is NVIDIA's Blackwell Ultra generation of data-centre GPU. The configuration, size and site are not decided: they depend on the demand and the quote described below.

Nothing has been bought. Fundraising is not open, and this page collects no money.

This initiative is part of we unfold, the home of the Open Intelligence movement: more people with the means to understand, build, influence and benefit from intelligence. Read the manifesto.

What participation means

Three different rights.

Using the cluster, deciding about it, and sharing in its economics are separate. None of them is settled yet.

Use
A defined, proportional share of an eligible pool of compute.
Still to define: the allocation period, what's in the pool, reservations, concurrency, priority, operating charges, downtime, and whether unused capacity expires, carries forward or can be reassigned. A share is not unlimited access and not a fixed number of tokens.
Governance
A recorded vote on specified decisions about this initiative.
Still to define: voting weight, quorum, delegation, limits on concentration, conflicts of interest, and the matters a simple capital majority can never override. It governs the compute initiative, not the Open Intelligence movement.
Economic participation
Any equity, revenue share, distribution or repayment.
Only under explicit terms, separate from use and votes. No promise of appreciation, liquidity, tradability or yield.

The operation

Know what keeps it running.

A shared operation deserves understandable accounts.

  1. Equipment

    GPUs, servers, storage and spare parts.

  2. Installation

    Racks, power distribution, setup and testing.

  3. Electricity

    For the GPUs, the servers around them, and the facility.

  4. Cooling

    Removing the heat all of that produces.

  5. Connectivity

    Getting work in and results out.

  6. Maintenance and operations

    The people, repairs and replacements that keep it working.

  7. Usable capacity

    What's left for real work after downtime, reservations and overhead.

Illustration, not a measurement

Follow one workload. A research group sends a batch of evaluation requests. They arrive over the network, the GPUs and servers do the work and draw electricity, cooling removes the heat, and the results go back. The cost of that task is a share of every layer above, including the hours the cluster sits idle: idle and reserved capacity still cost money. Unit costs will be counted on work actually delivered within the agreed quality and latency.

Cost assumptions
Each estimate will state its electricity price, utilization, maintenance, financing and replacement, with replacement counted once. Resale value won't be used to make the base case work.
Energy
Not measured yet. Will show GPU, server and facility consumption separately, each with its measurement boundary and period.
CO₂e
Not estimated yet. It will be an estimate, never a meter reading: a named emission factor, location and period. Embodied hardware impact shown separately when credible data exists.

The aim is to make capacity more accessible and more affordable as the operation improves. It isn't a promise to always be cheaper: electricity, utilization, service quality and maintenance all move the price. If owning it, keeping it private or controlling it costs more than a comparable external service, this page will say so.

Proof of Progress

Proceed, resize, or wait.

The cluster is bought only if it passes these gates, in order. Each one will show dated evidence when there is some.

  1. Understand demandNo evidence yet

    About 30 days of participant demand: workloads, concurrency, latency needs, cache behaviour and spend.

  2. Test on rented capacityNo evidence yet

    Compare task quality and service performance with realistic workloads before owning anything.

  3. Get a complete quoteNo evidence yet

    The full system and an operating budget, including site power and cooling.

  4. ValidateNo evidence yet

    Committed demand checked against a conservative operating scenario.

  5. Decide in publicNo evidence yet

    Publish the conditions to proceed, resize, delay or return funds.
