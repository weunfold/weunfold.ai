In one minute

Open models need somewhere to run.

The gap

Open-weight models are free to download, but the machines to run and train them are scarce and rented on terms few people see.

What we build

One 8× B300 node, installed in Brazil and run for 18 months, with its costs published.

What it does

Serves open-weight models through one API, and trains and fine-tunes open models whose weights and results are published.

Who shares it

The people and organisations who put it together share its compute, in proportion to what they contribute.

Where it goes

US$ 1M, three parts.

  1. 55%
    The machine · US$ 550k

    One 8× NVIDIA B300 node (2.3 TB of GPU memory), delivered, installed and acceptance-tested. Lowest public price found for the node: US$ 518,139.

  2. 36%
    Running it for 18 months · US$ 360k

    About US$ 20k a month: engineers on call, colocation and power in São Paulo, vendor support, bandwidth and spares. People, not electricity, are the largest cost.

  3. 9%
    Open training · US$ 90k

    People and time for fine-tuning and training open-weight models, published with their weights and results.

Proposed allocation. Prices from our August 2026 research; everything is re-quoted before anything is bought.

Why it can be affordable

Cheaper to make than to rent.

To produce
US$ 0.69–0.87per million output tokens on one 8-GPU Blackwell node, estimated from measured speed

US$ 1.98–3.96per million output tokens for the same class of model
Energy
~US$ 0.02per million tokens: staff, not power, dominate the cost

The gap is room to make access cheaper and to keep the operation running. It holds only while the machine is busy: idle capacity still costs money.

Research, August 2026. Prices and benchmarks move fast; they're re-checked before anything is bought.

How sharing works

Put it together. Share what it makes.

Proposal · terms in development
  1. Compute, in proportion

    Each participant's share of the cluster's capacity matches what they contribute.

  2. Unused capacity goes to others

    What participants don't use is offered to other people and projects who need it.

  3. Proceeds are shared too

    Proceeds from capacity sold to others are shared among participants in the same proportion.

  4. Participants decide about the cluster

    Decisions about this cluster stay with its participants. The Open Intelligence movement itself is not for sale.
